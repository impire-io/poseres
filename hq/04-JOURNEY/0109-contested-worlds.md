# Episode 0109 — Contested worlds: the rival that made the world easier (2026-08-16)

When a second body shares the world, is an attribution sense
necessary and sufficient to keep that world learnable? The topic was
born from the owner's question about multiple bodies co-habiting
(coding agents colliding on the same files, turf war vs
collaboration), pre-registered a four-rung ladder — invisible-peer
control, attribution sense, contention, scale — and died honestly on
rung 1, twice, in one day.

What measured true [measured]: a dedicated world (the N1 probe
config on its own container), the blessed 86-channel stack as
subject, and a per-step instrument settling the event head's
predicted delta against the realized observation. Solo baseline
0.021921. A scripted forager peer — dig melons, walk over drops,
every act logged — was worth **−37.9%** prediction error
(0.013615) and lifted food ≥ 12 time from 72% to 97%. Amendment 1,
registered before it ran, sent in an adversary instead: shadow the
subject, steal drops near it, dig the melon nearest it. The
adversary read **−25.3%** (0.016374). Both registered reversals
fired; Bar 1a failed even on its most charitable single channel
(mining, the one directly contended sense, +12.9% mean — real
unattributable interference, an order too small). The persisted
views discriminated the mechanism [measured]: the hostile subject
collected 2 drops in 15,075 steps, so windfall is not necessary —
**world-quieting is sufficient**: a peer that strips the standing
world leaves the distal senses reading mostly nothing, and nothing
is easy to predict. The forager's deeper drop is quieting plus
windfall [mechanism-argument].

What was refuted: the premise that co-habitation degrades
learnability and therefore licenses an others/attribution sense by
the senses-first rule. In this world, at this rung, a second body —
friend or rival — made the subject's world *more* predictable while
its meters stayed healthy. The turf war the topic was named for
never showed up in the head's numbers; it showed up only as a
fingerprint (mining) too small to clear any bar.

What it taught / what it opened: learnability is the wrong first
premise for multi-body senses in a renewable world — a consuming
peer empties the field faster than it complicates it. The successor
premise, written into the topic verdict for whoever reopens this: an
*outcome* premise (rung 3's territory — scarce, non-renewable
resources where the meters pay, not the head), or a world where a
peer's acts create structured surprise the subject must predict to
survive. The rig survives in git history: the two-body harness, the
scripted peer with forager/hostile/idle modes, the per-channel
prediction instrument, and the never-licensed rung-2 instrument
(`peers_reading.py`) — a D1-style reading for a peers channel in the
house grammar, ready the day a premise licenses it.

Graduated **to abandoned** — an abandoned topic is a result, and
this one is a finding: the first measured fact about bodies sharing
this project's worlds is that presence alone is not conflict
[measured, this world and rung only — judgment beyond it].

Reversal condition: if a future two-body reading shows the subject's
meters paying for the peer — eat completions or food ≥ 12 time
dropping ≥ 3× against solo on a non-renewable or admission-limited
food world — or shows prediction error rising ≥ 25% under a peer
whose acts add structure instead of removing it, contested-worlds
reopens at rung 2 with `peers_reading.py` as the frozen first
instrument.
